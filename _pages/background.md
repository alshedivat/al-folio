## My path: cognition, language, data, and human experience

My work began with a deep curiosity about the human mind, language, and the way people create meaning.

Literature was one of my first entry points into cognition. I have always been fascinated by the way stories, poems, metaphors, and images can move us, shape attention, and transform how we experience the world. This curiosity led me to study cognitive psychology, biology, and later to pursue doctoral research on metaphor appreciation, poetry reading, and aesthetic experience.

During my doctoral years, I explored how people perceive, interpret, and emotionally respond to literary language. I worked with experimental methods, eye-tracking, statistical modeling, and natural language processing tools to better understand the cognitive processes behind reading, attention, and meaning-making.

Over time, I became increasingly interested in applying these questions outside of academia. I transitioned toward data science, NLP, machine learning, and AI-powered tools, with the goal of making complex information easier to explore, understand, and use.

Today, my work connects behavioral analytics, cognitive science, artificial intelligence, and health research. I am especially interested in how data and technology can help us better understand human behavior, improve decision-making, and support more meaningful human experiences.

I am also committed to responsible and ethical technology. In my own work, I pay close attention to data privacy, interpretability, sustainability, and the social impact of AI systems.

This website is a place where I map out my evolving interests across data, cognition, health, and human change.
